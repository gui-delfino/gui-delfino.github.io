Welcome! I'm Gui (short for Guilherme and pronounced as "gui" in "guitar"). 


I am 5th-year PhD candidate in the Department of Physics at Purdue University working with Claudio Chamon in condensed matter theory. My research is focused on strongly correlated systems, quantum field theory, and quantum phases of matter. I transferred to Purdue from Boston University, where I spent four years before continuing my PhD with my advisor.


My research interests include both the theoretical study of quantum many-body systems and their realization in experimentally accessible platforms. On the theory side, I am interested in characterizing and classifying quantum phases using a broad range of tools, from numerical methods and quantum field theory to more abstract mathematical frameworks. At the same time, I am interested in proposing experimentally realistic settings in which such exotic phases and phenomena can be engineered and probed, with the broader goal of bridging the gap between theory and experiment.

 On this website you can find my [publications](https://gui-delfino.github.io/publications/), a list of my recent [talks](ttps://gui-delfino.github.io/talks/), as well as lecture notes of some [courses](https://gui-delfino.github.io/teaching/) I taught.


Research Interests
======
Among other topics, I'm highly interested in
* Effective field theories and continuum limits of microscopic lattice systems;
* Generalized notions of symmetries, particularly non-invertible, subsystem, and modulated symmetries, and how they give rise to rich structures in higher-dimensional systems;
* Topologically ordered systems, as FQHE, quantum spin liquids, and fractons
* Engineering quantum matter in mesoscopic systems
* Using long-range-ordered systems as synthetic platforms to probe physics in unconventional geometries and topologies.
